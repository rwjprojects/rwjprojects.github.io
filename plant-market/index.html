Each card below is one thing the data said that a person would not spot by scrolling tables — a price gap between the platforms, a term one shop has taken over, stock that is not moving, somebody new turning up. They are grouped by kind and sorted by how much to trust them: Confirmed means a second independent source agreed, Likely is one clear source, Weak is shown so you can look at it, not rely on it.

What these words mean

Confirmed / Likely / Weak — How much to trust a finding. Confirmed means a second independent source agreed — the other platform, or another day. Likely is one clear source. Weak is shown so you can look, not so you can rely on it.

Spread — The gap between the cheapest and dearest seller of the same plant. A big spread means the market has not settled on a price, which is where room usually is.

Named cultivar — A listing naming a specific variety, not just a genus — "Anthurium warocqueanum" rather than "anthurium". Used to check whether two platforms are really selling the same thing before calling a price gap arbitrage.

How this was collected

Where the numbers come from

Two live-selling platforms, Palmstreet and Whatnot, read directly from what they publish. Nothing here is a survey, a panel or an estimate: every figure is a count of something that was on one of those two sites at a stated moment. Where a number could not be measured it is left out rather than filled in.

When

Collected on 8 days between 4 Sep 2026 and 12 Sep 2026, across 193 saved files. The two halves are not equally fresh and the strip at the top of the page says which is which — live shows are re-read every few minutes, prices and sellers only when a collection is run.

Limitations — what this cannot tell you

  • These are floors, not censuses. Palmstreet serves live shows through a personalised 'for_you' feed -- every record carries section=for_you and there is no per-category live URL (five tried, all 404). So these counts are a FLOOR, not a census: they are what the feed shows an anonymous visitor, which may not be every show on the platform.
  • Listings are not sales. A shop posting most of the listings under a term holds the shelf space, not necessarily the revenue. Only the finished-show figures are money that changed hands.
  • A search matches text. A famously rare plant showing a low price usually means the search caught seedlings, cuttings or unrelated listings rather than the plant itself. Read a suspiciously cheap row twice.
  • Thin cells are marked, not hidden. A dagger means fewer than 10 finished shows behind a category, or fewer than 5 behind an hour. A trend line needs 4 readings before it is drawn at all; below that the reading count is shown instead.
  • One week is not a season. Nothing here can tell you whether a price is high for the year or high for this fortnight. That needs the collection to keep running, which is the cheapest thing on this list.
